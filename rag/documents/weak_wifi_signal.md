# Weak Wi-Fi Signal

## Description
Weak Wi-Fi signal occurs when the wireless client receives a relatively weak signal from the access point. Low signal strength can reduce link quality and may increase retransmissions, latency, or packet loss.

## Common Symptoms
- Low RSSI compared with the normal baseline.
- Unstable or reduced throughput.
- Intermittent connectivity at locations far from the access point.
- Increased latency or packet loss in poor coverage areas.

## Possible Causes
- Large distance between client and access point.
- Walls, floors, furniture, or other physical obstructions.
- Poor access-point placement.
- Radio-frequency interference.
- Poor antenna orientation or installation.

## Diagnostic Indicators
RSSI should be interpreted together with latency and packet loss. A low RSSI value with otherwise healthy connectivity can indicate coverage weakness, while low RSSI combined with packet loss provides stronger evidence of a degraded wireless link.

## Recommended Recovery Actions
1. Move the client closer to the access point and check whether RSSI and connectivity improve.
2. Reposition the access point to reduce physical obstructions.
3. Check for nearby sources of wireless interference.
4. Where available, use a better access-point location or additional coverage infrastructure.

## Verification
After the action, compare RSSI, latency, and packet loss with the values measured before recovery.

## Expected Outcome
Improved signal quality, lower packet loss, and more stable network performance.
