# Router Overheating

## Description
Router overheating refers to an abnormally elevated device temperature that may be associated with instability, reduced performance, or unexpected restarts. Temperature alone does not prove that overheating is the cause of a network fault.

## Common Symptoms
- Temperature remains above the deployment's validated operating range.
- Device instability or unexpected restarts.
- Performance degradation that correlates with elevated temperature.
- Increased network errors during periods of high device temperature.

## Possible Causes
- Poor ventilation.
- Blocked airflow around the device.
- High sustained processing or traffic load.
- Environmental temperature that is too high.
- Hardware or cooling-related problems.

## Diagnostic Indicators
Use temperature together with network telemetry and time correlation. A temperature increase that consistently coincides with network instability is more informative than a single high reading.

## Recommended Recovery Actions
1. Ensure that the router has adequate airflow and is not enclosed by objects that block ventilation.
2. Reduce unnecessary network load when possible.
3. Check whether instability disappears after the device returns to its normal operating range.
4. If overheating persists, inspect the device according to the manufacturer's maintenance procedure.

## Verification
Monitor temperature and network performance after corrective action. Confirm that temperature stabilizes and that the associated network symptoms do not recur.

## Expected Outcome
Stable device temperature and improved network reliability.

## Safety Note
Do not open or modify powered networking equipment unless the work is authorized and performed using appropriate safety procedures.
